Landmark motion must read as two continuous rows of stationary buildings receding past the viewer while the camera travels forward—not as individual images launching from the edges and zooming toward the viewer. Keep several buildings visible at different depths on each side, use one shared vanishing-point trajectory and camera speed, delay perspective growth until the near field, and let near buildings occlude far buildings before they pass outside the viewport.

Each side is a tightly packed opaque architectural wall, not scattered individual landmarks: all visible assets for that side stay in the same continuous perspective queue, share one ground/baseline trajectory, overlap without gaps, remain fully opaque, and run from the 15%/85% vanishing boundary toward the outer lower corner.

Build the landmark motion from six unique 2.5D district assets on each side rather than fifty isolated single-building assets. Each district contains four or five overlapping landmarks at varied height and depth plus one shared irregular kraft-paper ground plane, low ridges, and contact shadows; the complete district travels and scales as one perspective unit. Reuse those six assets across enough evenly spaced world-depth instances to keep the projected streetscape continuously connected from far to near. Never fade, blur, or mask the landmark artwork. Do not add a fixed full-width ground overlay; the central reading field stays quiet and clear.

District composition, cropping, terrain, roads, ridges, vegetation, and contact shadows must be produced as one raster asset through the approved image-to-image generation workflow. Do not reconstruct or supplement district ground with CSS gradients, pseudo-elements, `clip-path`, polygons, ellipses, or other programmatic shapes. The page only positions, scales, and animates the finished transparent district images.

Each side reuses its six generated district assets across 84 evenly phased world-depth instances. Use adaptive hard visibility rather than opacity: render every fifth sample in the far field, every third sample in the middle field, and every sample only in the final near field. This keeps distant and middle architecture loose while concentrating enough depth samples near the camera to prevent a scale discontinuity when the nearest ground plane exits. District anchors follow one shared perspective ray from the 15%/85% vanishing boundary toward the lower outer edge. Drive all actors from one `requestAnimationFrame` camera clock: world depth decreases at a constant rate while projected scale and screen displacement are calculated reciprocally from `1 / Z`. Calculate the exit distance from the rendered district width and viewport anchor instead of a fixed `vw`, so the nearest actor completes its exit while the following ground plane remains visible on desktop and narrow split-view layouts. Do not animate `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`, opacity, or filter; update only visibility, `translate3d(...) scale(...)`, and depth order.

The approved replacement geometry uses one complete, continuous, direction-specific street master for each side. Adjacent segments therefore preserve the original road and terrain across their shared boundary. At the loop boundary, rotate segment assignments only at geometrically identical positions. The left lane may load only the left-receding master and the right lane only the right-receding master. Do not reintroduce independently generated trapezoid modules, mixed-direction assets, or nested copies of the entire unsegmented master.
